Overview for Tutor Only 
Ifyou are working with an adult, be sure to give your student the 
Pretest BEFORE starting this lesson. 
Since you are starting in Lesson 1, you either have an older student with very limited exposure to letters, oryou are working with a young child. 
In this lesson, you '11 introducejust one vowel, A, and you will only 
teach its Short sound. You' 11 show your student how to tap the 
keyword Apple. 
You'll also introduce six consonants. Ifyour student knows the 
sound the consonant makes, do NOT teach its keyword. Only 
teach a keyword ifthe student does not know ( or is not confident 
of) the sound. Ifyou do teach a keyword, write the letter and its keyword on a piece ofpaper, and have the student draw a picture 
to remind him ofthe keyword. Keep that paper visible throughout 
this lesson and future lessons until that letter's sound is automatic. 
Watch for confusion between the B and P letter or sound. Ifyou notice confusion, stop and teach the trick of"Balloons go up in the air, pigs go down inthe mud." 
Since each procedure in this lesson is new, go slowly. Teach the procedure as carefully as you teach the letter sounds. 
You may finish the entire lesson in one sitting. Oryou may have to spend several sessions on a single lesson. It doesn't matter. The rule is: do notgo onto the next lesson until the letters taught in this onecan be accurately and easily read and spelled. 
Don't rush your stu~ent. You canrepeat this lesson using the words in the ForA Repeat Lesson section at the end ofeach step.

Overview for Tutor Only 
Ifyour student started with Lessons 1, 2, 3 or 4, the procedures in the lesson will be familiar. No new procedures are introduced in the rest ofthis book. Ifyou have a Keyword Page, bring it out at the very beginning ofthe lesson and keep it out the entire time. Ifnew keywords are taught, add them to the existing page. 
Ifyou are working with an adult, and the pretest results showed that this should be his first lesson, it means your student already knows the short sound for A, I, 0 and U, so don't b.other to teach him those keywords. But he will need a keyword for the vowel taught in this lesson-the E. Since each procedure in this lesson will be new, go slowly. Teach the procedure as carefully as you teach the letter sounds. 
In this lesson, you will need to explain the reason for a Digraph. There are 44 sounds in our language but only 26 letters. So some letters "pair up" to represent a sound. When two letters make just one sound, they'll both be on the same tile. They're called a Digraph. 
When you put your tiles in alphabetical order, mix the vowels in with the consonants. But put the digraphs in a separate row above the alphabet. This helps emphasize them and makes them easier to find. 
Many students have difficulty hearing the difference between /ch/ 
and /sh/. So you '11 teach that the difference is how long the sound ' 
lasts. You can hang onto /sh/ for a long time, but/ch/ is a very fast 
sound. You'll also teach hand signals for these sounds. 
This is the last lesson in the book. To ensure that your student has 
mastered all the essential skills, you should give him the Posttest at 
your next session. 
Do not go on to Book 3 until your student can easily and accurately 
do all the tasks on the Posttest. 
SUMMARY: 
• 
Ifthis is student's first lesson, don't teach keyword for A, I, 0, or U. 

• 
Teach the concept of Digraph. 

• 
Watch out for /ch/ /sh/ confusion. 

• 
After this lesson, give the Posttest to ensure mastery.

A: Rhyming Game  
Students leatned how to rhyme in the last lesson ofBook 1. Most students enjoy coming up with rhymes, now that they know how.  
To do this activity, bring out the BLANK tiles. Parent/tutor puts out correct number oftiles for the first word. Parent/Tutor touches and says the first word, then touches and says each sound.  
Student changes the first tile and creates the first rhyming word. Student says the sound ofthat first tile, then says the new word.  
Parent/Tutor changes the second tile and creates the second rhy1ning word. Repeat this process for as long as possible.  
Parent/Tutor can give hints, such as ''Would (sound) work?"  
Variation: ifyour child/student has a difficult time coming up with real words, allow him to change the first tile, says its sound, say the new word, and then tell you whether it is a Real word or a Nonsense word.  
Here are some good rhyming words that need 3 tiles: FAN: ban, Dan, man, pan, ran, tan, van, etc. RJCH: ditch, hitch, Mitch, Niche, pitch, ditch, witch, etc. PIT: bit, fit, hit, kit, sit, etc. (watch out for SH) DASH: bash, cash, gash, hash, mash, nash, etc. MAD: bad, sad, tad, lad, etc. BILL: fill, will,pill, chill, etc. LAP: cap, gap, map, tap, chap, etc. DID: bid, hid, kid, lid, Sid, etc. BIG: dig, fig,jig, mig, pig, etc. BUN: done, fun, gun, hon, none, pun, sun, ton, etc.  
Let yourimaginationrun wild. Try to use words that have SHORT vowel sounds and are in a Consonant-Vowel-Consonant pattern.
