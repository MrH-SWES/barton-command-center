Overview for Tutor Only 
In the last lesson, students learned when to spell the /k/ sound with a C and when to spell it with aKat (ornear) the beginning ofa word. 
In this lesson, you'11 teach the rules for spelling /kl at the end ofa word, using the Mille Truck rule. 
In a word with only 1 vowel, the /kl sound at the end will NEVER be spelled with aC. 
So at the end ofa word with only I vowel, your choice is Kor CK. Only use CKright after a Short vowel, as in Truck. (K can never come right after a short vowel.) How do he know the vowel is Short? It's in a Closed syllable. 
Ifyou can't spell /kl with a CK, use a K. 
When you add the Mille Truck rule to their list ofspelling rules, be sure to put the breve (teacup) over the U in truck to remind them to only use CK right after a short vowel. 
InNew Teaching #2, you 'II show there is one exception to the Milk Truck rule. Words that end in /kt/, such as fact, are always spelled CT. 
Sprinkle these questions through the lesson after you've taught the new material: 
Why did you spell it C-K? 
Why did they spel1 /k/ with a K? 
How will you always spell /kt/? 
SUMMARY: 

• 
Teach spelling rules for /kl at the end ofa word with only one vowel. 

• 
/kl at end ofa word with only one vowel will never be a C. 

• 
Spell it CK only ifit comes right after a Short vowel. 



Otherwise, spell it K. 
• 
This is the Milk Truck rule. 

• 
Words that end in /kt/ are always spelled CT, such as Fact.

A: Overview for Tutor Only 
So far, your studenthas learned only one unit: ALL. 
Units are a group of3 letters that appear at the endofa word and 
make aslightly differentsound as a group than they would 
individually. It's important that your student rapidly recognize those 
3 letters as a Unit. 
Units are NOT the same as Digraphs. In a Digraph, such as TH, 
both letters make only l sound. In a Unit, each letter makes a 
sound. Theyjust make a different sound as a Unit than they would 
individually. ' 
In this lesson, your student will learn two sets ofUnits: those ending with NG (ang, ing, ong, ung), and those ending with NK (ank, ink, onk, and unk). 
The most common spelling mistake is to spell ING or f.NK with an E instead ofan I. So you'll emphasize that there is NO ENG or ENK tile. It's the one vowel that is missing from these Uruts. (ENG only occurs in four words-length, strength, English, andEngland­and those four words will be taught as Sight words in a later 
less<,>~.) 
As before, students will markUnit syllables by drawing a box around the Unit. 
Sprinkle these questions through the lesson afteryou've taught the new material: 
Where is the Unit in that word? 
What kind ofsyllable is that-Closed or Unit?

Introduce Units with NG ANG, ING, ONG, UNG.

Introduce Units with NK ANK, ~ ONK, UNK.

Overview for Tutor Only

Overview for Tutor Only 
Because people are always in a huny, we have created ways to speak faster. Contractions are one ofthose ways. 
Contractions are two separate words that have been squished together to form just one word, which allows us to ta1kfaster. 
Here are the rules you'11 teach yourstudents: 
The first word in a contraction never changes. You only change the 
second word. In the second word, you leave out the first vowel and any letters in frontofit. 
You put an apostrophe where you left out the letter(s ). 
Then you squish the first and second words together. 
There are a few contractions that don't follow those rules. You'll teach those "weirdo" contractions in the NewTeaching#2 procedure. •

Materials Needed: 

I
NEW Units: OLL, OLD, OLT, OST, ILD, IND Letter Tiles: A through Z plus F, L, S, Z, and CH, TH, CK, SH, WH, TCH Units: ALL, ANG, ING, ONG, UNG, ANK, INK, ONK, UNK Binder Paper and Pencil Word Frame Blank Colored 5x8 Index Card

Overvielv for Tutor Only 
A few lessons ago, your student learned his first Unit: ALL. Then he learned the ING and INK Units. Today, he'll learn hls last set of Units for quite a while. 
Units always come at the end ofa word, always consists of3 letters, and those three letters make an unusual sound. In each of today's Units, the vowel makes its Long sound. 
Since your student may not know what a Long sound is, you'll say "the vowel says its own name." So a vowel will either say its Short sound or its Own Name.

Student Lacks Fluency (Speed) 

It is more important that your student be accurate than fast. However, ifyour student has to sound out many ofthe words in phrases orsentences, or even after reading the story to himself, still has to stop and sound out words, then work on fluency. 
Research shows that repeate~ readings will eventually improve accuracy. So on phrases, have a student read the Who phrases out loud. Then randomly point to various Who phrases and have him read them to you. Do this until each phrase bas been read at least 3 times, and hopefully, the student can read them without having to sound out each word. 
Then do the same thing for the Did What phrases. Then give your student even more practice by having him create lots of sentences that contain justWho and Did What phrases. Continue that process with the Where and Add-On phrases. 
Vv1len reading sentences, make sure your student is reading each sentence at least four times: once to himself, once out loud, once again while marking phrases, and once more with good phrasing. 
When reading stories, you may be able to get students to read each story aloud several times by using a stopwatch and timing him doing a Cold, Hot, and Blazing reading. But NEVER sacrifice accuracy for speed. 
Start by having the student read the story to himself. Call the first time he reads it aloud a Cold reading, and time him. Also keep track ofthe number oferrors. Write down the time and errors. Say something like, 0 Now that you 'rewarmed up, let's try a Hot reading." Have him read it again out loud while timing him. Write down his new time and errors. Hopefully, both will have gone down. 
Ifyour studentwill tolerate it, ask, "Are you ready for aBlazing 
reading?" and time him reading it a third time. Write down his 
new time, which hopefully, will have no errors at all. 

Congratulate him on his Blazing time. (You can even chart it, if 
you wish.) 

Repeated readings will help your student become a more fluent reader-but it takes time. You will not see instant improvement.
