Overview for Tutor Only

Your student is ready to tackle words with two syllables. You can
tell a word has two syllables because it has two vowels that are not
next to each other.

To divide a word into syllables, first locate the vowels. Drag each
vowel into its own syllable.

Next, count the number of consonants (blue tiles) between the
vowels. In this lesson, there will only be | blue tile between the
vowels,

Start by moving that tile to the end of the word, which will make the
first syllable Open. Your student should try that option first. If that
creates a real word, he’s done. If it doesn’t create a real word, he
should move that blue tile the other way, which will close off that
first syllable and make its vowel short.

Several of the procedures in this lesson have been modified because
we're now working with multi-syllable words. For instance, when
spelling, your student will say the whole word, tap the table while
saying cach syllable, then say and spell one syllable at a time.

You'll teach that Y at the end of a word says /ee/ as in Baby. You'll
also point out that multi-syllable words don’t use the FLOSS
spelling rule. The FLOSS rule only applies to words that have one
vowel, as does the word FLOSS. And C switches to /s/ whether
the Watch Out vowel makes its Short or Long sound.

Overview for Tutor Only

In this lesson, you’! teach your student how to spell /k/ in a multi-
syllable word.

You’ll start with /k/at the end ofa word. The Milk Truck rule still
applies unless the ending is /ic/. In multi-syllable words, /ic/ is
always spelled I-C. This is called the Music Trick spelling rule.

Then you'll show your student that the Kiss the Cat rule still applies
to the beginning of a word.

But in the middle ofa multi-syllable word, your student must
combine those two rules. Your student should spell /k/ in the middle
with aC if he can, a CK if /k/ is right after a Short vowel, or else a
K. This is called the Picnic Chicken Basket rule.

Sprinkle these questions throughout the lesson after you've
taught the new matenal:

* Where’s the /k/ sound? So which rule will you use?

* What should you try first? Then what?
