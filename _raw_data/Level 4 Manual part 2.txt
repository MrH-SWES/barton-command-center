Overview for Tutor Only 
In most two-syllables words, the first syllable is accented. The 
vowel in the accented syllable is always pronounced clearly and 
correctly. 
But in order to speak quickly, the vowel in the UN-accented 
syllable (usually the second syllable), is often pronounced as a 
Short-U. That sound is called Schwa. 
This happens most often in unaccented Closed syllables that have an A or 0. It also happens in multi-syllable words that end with any vowel followed by an L. 
You 'II be teaching the Schwa both for reading and spelling. When 
reading an unknown word, your student should first pronounce it 
exactly as written. But ifit sounds a bit odd, he should change the 
vowel sound in the unaccented syllable into a Schwa. 
When spelling, ifa student hears a Schwa sound in the unaccented syllable, he should use his Electronic Spell Checker to double­check its speJiing. 
From now on, leave the Electronic Spell Checker where your student can easily reach it. Encourage him to use it whenever he isn't positive about the spelling ofa word. 
Sprinkle these questions throughout the lesson after you've taught the new material: 
• 
Which vowel is making the Schwa sound? 

• 
Do you hear a Schwa in that word? 


SUMMARY: 
• Accenting 
In most two-syllable words, the first syllable is accented. 
• Schwa 
The vowel in an unaccented syllable says /uh/ instead ofits sound. This allows us to talk faster. 
• Reading Schwa 
An A or O in a closed unaccented syllable will make the Schwa sound. 
Ifthe last syllable ends with any vowel, then an L, the vowel will make the Schwa sound. 
• Spelling Schwa 
Ifyou hear /uh/ in the unaccented syllable, use an Electronic Spell Checker to determine which vowel is making that sound.

In lesson 6, your student learned that in a two-syllable word, the last sylJable is usuaJly not accented. And a Closed-A orO in the unaccented syllable changes to the Schwa sound of/uh/. 
In this lesson, you' II teach that an Open-A that is not accented will also change to Schwa. 
In New Teaching# 1, you'JI demonstrate this in two-syllable words. In most two-syllable words, the last syllable is not accented. 
You'II teach that an Open-A at theend ofa word will always change to Schwa (as in plasma), and an Open-A by itself at the beginnfog will usually change to Schwa (as in adopt). 
In New Teaching #2, you'11 demonstrate this in three•syllabJe words. In three-syllable words, either the first orsecond syllable is accented-never the third. So an Open-A at the end will always change to Schwa. 
Since only one syllable can be accented, ifa word has two unaccented Open-A syllables, both will change to Schwa (as in Banana and Canada). 
Soyou'll teach a new Spelling rule, called the Banana Rule. Ifa student hears /uh/ at the end ofan unaccented syllable, it,vill be an Open-A that has changed to Schwa. 
Be sure to have the rubber bands handy throughout this lesson to demonstrate accenting.

• In three•sylJable words, the last syllable is never accented. 
Usually the first syllable is accented, but sometimes it is the second syllable.

Only one syllabic in a word is accented. So ifa three-syllable word has two Open-A syBabies that are not accented. both ofthem will change to Schwa. (banan~ Canada)
