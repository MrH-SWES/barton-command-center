Overview of Each Lesson 
Your student will get additional practice reading longer words. But in this book, the words will be longer because they have prefixes or suffixes added onto a Baseword. 
The first 7 lessons teach the meaning ofsuffixes and the two major spelling rules that affect suffixes: the Doubling rule and the Change 
rule. 
The last 3 lessons present 12 ofthe most common prefixes, while 
continuing to have suffixes appear at the end ofwords. By the end ofthis book, your student should no longer leave suffixes offofwords when reading or spelling. but be much better able to figure ·out the meaning oflong, unknown words, which will increase his reading comprehension.

Overview for Tutor Only 
In the last lesson, your student learned his first consonant suffix-S. 
A consonant suffix is one that starts with a consonant. 
In this lesson, he 'IIlearn five more consonant suffixes. The trickiest suffix is FUL, which is spelled with only one L. LESS and NESS are also tricky, as they both end in two S's, even though they are 
not accented. 
You'll show yourstudent how to combine the meaning ofthe Baseword withthe meaning ofthe suffix to figure out the meaning of the entire word. This is the first step in helping him expand his vocabulary. 
In NewTeaching#1, your student will addjust one suffix onto a Baseword. In NewTeaching #2, you '11 showhim that Basewords can have two suffixes at the same time. 
It isimportant that your student learn to recognize when a long word has a suffix at the end, and to be able to figure out just the Baseword. 
Sprinkle these questions throughout the lesson after you've taught the new material: 
• 
What's the Baseword? 

• 
How many suffixes in that word? 


SUMl\1ARY: 
• 
Consonant suffixes start with a consonant. 

• 
Suffix FUL is spelled with only one L. LESS and NESS have two S's. 

• 
A word may have one or two suffixes. 

• 
Make sure yom student can always figure out the Baseword.

Overview for Tutor Only 
In this lesson, your student will learn the two most common suffixes in our language: ED and ING. 
In New Teaching# 1, you'11 teach that adding ED means something 
happened in the past, while adding ING means something is 
happening right now. You'll also show that adding a Vowel suffix 
adds another syllable to the Baseword, so you must apply syllable 
division rules. 
In New Teaching #2, you'll use Basewords that need the Doubling rule. Ifa one-syllable word ends with a Consonant-Vowel­Consonant, and you 're adding a Vowel su.flb4 then you must double the last letter before adding a Vowel suffix. An example would be tap-tapping (not taping). 
Ifthe Baseword is a two-syllable word, you only need to use the Doubling rule ifthe last syllable is accented and ends in Consonant­Vowel-Consonant, such as beginning and regrett~ but not happening. 
Sprinkle these questions through the lesson after you've taught the new material: 
Does it need the Doubling Rule? 
What would it say ifyou had forgotten to double that letter? 
SUMMARY: 
• Suffix ED and ING tell when the action took place 
ED means it happened in the 
past 
ING means it is happening right now. 
• Doubling Rule: 
Ifa one-syllable word ends with eve, double the final letter before adding a Vowel suffix.. (tapping, taping) 
Ifa two-syllable word ends with eve and the second syllable is accent~ double the final letter before adding a Vowel suffix. (regrett~ beginning)
