Overview for Tutor Only 
In this lesso°' your student will learn the second spelling rule that 
affects suffixes: the Change Y to I rule. 
In New Teaching #I, your student will learn that ifa Baseword ends in Y, the Y must be changed to I before adding any suffix, even a 
Consonant suffix. 
Also, to make it plural, you must add ES (puppy, puppies). That's 
because ifyou just added S, you would make the final syllable 
closed and change the vowel to a short sound (puppis). 
In NewTeaching #2, your student will learn there are two times he will not change a Y to an I. Ifthe suffix itself starts with an I, you do not need to change the Y because in our language, words cannot have two I's in a row. ( cry, cried, crying) 
Also, ifthe Y is part ofa Vowel Team, you do not change it. (play, plays, played) 
Sprinkle these questions through the lesson after you've taught the new material: 
Does anything need to be changed? 
Why wouldn't you change the Y? 
SUMMARY: 
• 
If the Baseword ends in Y, change Y to I before adding any suffix. (copier, plentiful) 

• 
To make it plural, add ES. not just S. (puppy, puppies) 

• 
But don't change Y if: 


Suffix starts with an I (cry, cries, crying) 
Or the Y is part of a Vowel Team (play, played, playing)

Overview for Tutor Only 
In the final three lessons ofthis book, your student will learn 12 prefixes. Prefixes come at the beginning ofa wor~ and each prefix has a specific meaning. Bycombining the prefix's meaning with the Baseword's meaning, your student can .figure out the meaning ofthe word, and start to increase his vocabulary. 
In this Jesso~ all four prefixes you'll be teaching mean the same thing-NOT. 
The goal in learning the meaning ofprefixes is to allow your student to expand his vocabulary. When he comes across words he doesn't know, ifhe can identify the Baseword, and ifhe lmows the meaning ofthe prefix, he will often be able to figureout the meaning ofthe entire word. This will increase his reading comprehension 
A secondary goal is to reduce the panic some students feel when they see a Jong word. Many long words have a prefix, a suffix, or both. Ifthe student looks for them, and can then focus onjust the Baseword, long words won't be as scary. 
SprinkJe these questions through the lesson after you've taught the new material: 
What's just the Baseword? 
So what does that entire word mean?

DIS, IN, UN, and NON all mean the same thing· not

Your student now knows eight prefixes. Four mean Not-DIS. fN, UN, NON-plus MIS, SUB, RE, and PRE. 
In this lesson, he'll leam four more prefixes that all indicate a directiort INTER means Between, MID means Middle, OVER means Over the Top (or too much), and UP means Up. 
UP is tricky. Sometimes you must put it behind the Baseword to figure out what it means. Forexample, upstairs means going up the stairs, but upkeep means keeping up. 
The goal in learning the meaning ofprefixes is to allow your student to expand his vocabulary. When he comes across words he doesn't know, ifhe can isolate the Basewor~ and he !mows the meaning of the prefix, he will often be able to figure out the meaning ofthe word. This will increase his reading comprehension.
